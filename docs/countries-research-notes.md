# Countries research and editorial decision log

Research reviewed on 25 November 2026. This is a working editorial record, not a claim that every local custom has been independently reviewed by a resident. Country guides should be reviewed by a knowledgeable local editor before expanding or making nuanced claims.

## Countries marked ready

### India — ready

- Occasions checked: Diwali and Holi descriptions and usual season: [Incredible India — Fairs and Festivals](https://www.prod.incredibleindia.gov.in/content/incredible-india-v2/en/experiences/art/fairs-and-festivals.html). Eid al-Fitr timing, meaning, gatherings and greeting: [Incredible India — Eid ul-Fitr](https://www.incredibleindia.gov.in/en/festivals-and-events/eid-ul-fitr).
- Messaging channel evidence: [Mobile Ecosystem Forum — Business Messaging Customer Survey 2024](https://mobileecosystemforum.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Business-Messaging-Costumer-Survey-2024.pdf), India results identify WhatsApp as the leading P2P channel among respondents surveyed. This is a survey result, not a live census of every user.
- Human check: re-check the MEF survey's sample and methodology before adding any percentage or implying universal use. No percentage is used in the guide. Regional and household practices vary; the page deliberately avoids presenting a single set of rituals or language as universal.

No app is named: available Nepal Messenger figures were advertising-reach estimates, not confirmed active messaging use.

## Cross-link methodology

Draft country pages link to the six geographically nearest country pages using approximate country reference points and great-circle distance. These are navigation suggestions, not formal statements about shared borders; island and microstate results should receive a local editorial check. Ready guide pages use same-region links, prioritising recorded land-border neighbours where available.

## Bangladesh guide — source checks for the November 2026 update

Bangladesh was added as a ready English guide after checking the following country-specific sources. The page uses broad seasonal or calendar timing and does not publish exact Gregorian festival dates.

- **Pohela Boishakh / Mangal Shobhajatra:** Bangladesh Tourism Board's culture-and-festivals page describes Pahela Baishakh as the Bengali New Year and records public cultural activity. UNESCO's Intangible Cultural Heritage entry describes the Dhaka University Faculty of Fine Art procession, its public nature, masks/floats and symbolism of solidarity. Sources: [Bangladesh Tourism Board](https://tourismboard.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/6922e0d4933eb65569e28c18), [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage](https://ich.unesco.org/en/RL/mangal-shobhajatra-on-pahela-baishakh-01091).
- **Eid al-Fitr:** Bangladesh's Ministry of Foreign Affairs lists Eid al-Fitr among major festivals. Dhaka Tribune's Bangladesh coverage documents congregational prayers, greetings, family meals, visits and gift exchange. Sources: [Bangladesh MOFA](https://oicpm.mofa.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/culture-of-bangladesh-p51h0k-6a0c4dc15d40ccedfd8f0156), [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/377674/bangladesh-celebrates-eid-ul-fitr-with-prayers).
- **Eid al-Adha:** Dhaka Tribune's 2025 Bangladesh report records prayers, family gatherings and meat distribution to people in need. The guide does not imply that every Muslim household performs qurbani. Source: [Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/383338/eid-ul-azha-congregations-held-peacefully-across).
- **Durga Puja:** Bangladesh's tourism authority describes the festival as an important Hindu religious and cultural event in Bangladesh and notes that people from different backgrounds may attend public celebrations. The official MOFA culture page lists Durga Puja among festivals observed by Hindus. Sources: [Bangladesh Tourism Authority](https://beautifulbangladesh.gov.bd/district-destination/dhaka/events/80), [Bangladesh MOFA](https://central.mofa.gov.bd/pages/static-pages/bangladeshi-culture-and-events-492130-6952669c35ce18e1c05aa566).
- **Victory Day:** The Armed Forces Division describes the occasion as commemorating the 1971 Liberation War victory. The guide uses the broad period “mid-December” rather than a numeric date. Source: [Armed Forces Division](https://afd.gov.bd/index.php/media/important-occations/victory-day).
- **Messaging platforms:** The 2024 Survey of the Bangladeshi People by The Asia Foundation and BRAC Institute of Governance and Development reports IMO, Facebook, WhatsApp and Messenger among the leading communication platforms used by respondents with internet access. The page deliberately describes this as survey evidence, not a claim about every person. Source: [The Asia Foundation — 2024 survey PDF](https://asiafoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Bangladesh_The-State-of-Bangladeshs-Political-Governance-Development-and-Society-According-to-its-Citizens-2024_Survey-of-the-Bangladeshi-People.pdf), section 8, Table 8.3.

**Editorial caution:** The Bangladesh guide is marked ready based on the cited sources, but the Bengali version is an AI draft and remains `reviewed: false`. A Bangladesh-based native speaker should confirm the names and transliteration of Bengali festival terms, especially the preferred written form of Pohela Boishakh, and review whether the wording on qurbani, Durga Puja and Victory Day is suitably sensitive across communities.

### Bangladesh messaging-source qualification

The Asia Foundation / BRAC Institute of Governance and Development 2024 survey reports that, among respondents with internet access, the four leading communication platforms were IMO, Facebook, WhatsApp and Messenger (section 8.1.1, printed page 124; it gives respondent percentages of 68.4%, 56.1%, 53.6% and 44.6% respectively). The country guide names the platforms but does not repeat percentages. This should not be read as a national census or as proof that a particular recipient uses any app. Text extraction of the PDF was available, but the PDF screenshot service returned an anti-bot verification screen rather than the page image; a human editor should visually check the table in the source PDF before any numeric claim is introduced.


## Pakistan guide — source checks for the November 2026 update

Pakistan was promoted to ready after reviewing country-specific material on religious occasions, greeting etiquette, national occasions and communication channels. The page uses usual calendar timing rather than exact Gregorian festival dates.

- **Eid al-Fitr and regional celebrations:** Pakistan's Press Information Department describes Eid as a celebration after Ramadan and notes Eidi, regional cuisines and clothing as expressions of the country's diversity. Source: [Pakistan Press Information Department](https://pid.gov.pk/site/press_detail/32194).
- **Eid al-Adha:** Radio Pakistan's 2026 coverage describes Eid prayers and qurbani, while Dawn's feature explains family and neighbourhood gatherings, meat sharing and why households should not compete over the size or cost of sacrifice. Sources: [Radio Pakistan](https://www.radio.gov.pk/27-05-2026/nation-celebrates-eid-ul-azha-today-with-religious-zeal), [Dawn](https://www.dawn.com/news/1699011).
- **Pakistan Day and Independence Day:** The Press Information Department's Pakistan Day message explains the historical connection to the Lahore Resolution and the establishment of Pakistan. Sources: [PID — Pakistan Day 2026](https://pid.gov.pk/site/press_detail/32196), [PID — Pakistan Day 2025](https://pid.gov.pk/site/press_detail/28385).
- **Greetings and regional languages:** The Cultural Atlas entry authored by Pakistani researchers describes greeting elders first, formal greetings, the use of “Assalamu alaikum” among Muslim Pakistanis, and Pakistan's linguistic diversity including Urdu, Punjabi, Sindhi, Pashto, Balochi and Saraiki. Source: [Mosaica / The Cultural Atlas — Pakistani greetings](https://theculturalatlas.org/pakistani-culture/pakistani-culture-greetings). The Pakistan Multicultural Association also documents “Assalamu alaikum”, “Walaikum assalam”, “Shukriya” and “Eid Mubarak” in community use. Source: [Pakistan Multicultural Association — Cultural Guide](https://www.pakistanmulticultural.org.au/resources/cultural-guide).
- **Messaging channel evidence:** Gallup & Gilani Pakistan surveyed 769 men and women in urban and rural areas from 28 June to 10 July 2024. The survey question asked which social-networking websites respondents used to stay in touch with friends and loved ones; WhatsApp was named most often, followed by Facebook. The guide therefore calls WhatsApp a practical option, not a universal or census-level claim. Source: [Gallup & Gilani Pakistan survey](https://gallup.com.pk/wp/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/09.08.2024.Daily-poll-English-1.pdf).

**Editorial caution:** The English guide deliberately avoids making broad claims about religious identity, handshakes or language preference. A Pakistan-based reviewer should check the balance across regions and communities. The Urdu translation is an AI-assisted draft and remains `reviewed: false`; it must not be indexed until an Urdu-speaking reviewer confirms the meaning, tone, spelling and cultural sensitivity.
