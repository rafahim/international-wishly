## Search-language research notes

- Competitor messaging repeatedly describes the product as a way to make a small website/page for someone special, then explains a pick → personalise → share flow. This supports natural phrases such as “make a website for someone you love”, “personal website gift”, and “shareable page”.
- Recipient and occasion pages are useful because a visitor may search for a birthday page for a partner, a long-distance message, a proposal page, an online invitation, or a digital love letter rather than a generic website builder.
- Love-letter comparison pages observed search phrasing including “best digital love letter website”, “create a digital love letter” and “digital love letter website”.
- Template and maker intent is separated from editorial intent: collection pages help visitors browse designs; Insights articles answer planning questions and writing problems; the main Wishly domain handles account and editor tasks.
- These are search phrases and observed intent patterns, not measured monthly search volume, keyword difficulty or guaranteed ranking opportunities. Use Google Search Console and Keyword Planner after launch to validate country-level impressions and clicks.

## Protecting both domains from duplication

- `wishly.rafahim.com` self-canonicalizes each international page to its own URL.
- `wishly.bd` is the main product and account destination. The international About page clearly explains this relationship.
- Keep unique body copy, page titles, descriptions, examples and FAQs on each domain. Avoid publishing the same guide or collection text verbatim on both domains.
- Do not point every international canonical to wishly.bd. That would signal that the main domain, not the international page, is the preferred version.
- Do not add hreflang between pages that are not true language/region equivalents. If translated equivalents are later created, add matching hreflang annotations.
- Keep sitemap URLs consistent with self-canonical URLs, and use server redirects for any old duplicate paths.

## Technical SEO checklist

- Every public page has a page-specific `<title>`, meta description, canonical, Open Graph title/description/image, and Twitter card.
- The sitemap includes the core site pages, occasion collections, template details, all published Insights articles and the country directory/detail pages. Its contents are generated from the current route data; do not rely on this document for a fixed route count.
- Robots allows public crawling and references the sitemap.
- Structured data is page-specific: WebSite/Organization, BreadcrumbList, CollectionPage/ItemList, BlogPosting, HowTo, AboutPage, and FAQ where the page visibly contains the same questions and answers.
- Keep images crawlable and alt text descriptive; use the actual design name rather than keyword-stuffed alt text.
- After deployment, verify the sitemap and canonical URLs in Google Search Console. Inspect representative home, category, template, Insights and country URLs.
